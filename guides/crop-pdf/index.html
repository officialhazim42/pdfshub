Tool Overview & Requirements

Crop PDF adjusts the visible page bounds with top, bottom, left, and right margins. It can apply the change to all, odd, or even pages.

Input One PDF
Output A cropped PDF
Requirements One readable PDF is required. Margin values are points and should leave a positive page area.

Step-by-Step Instructions

Follow these essential instructions to achieve optimal results when using Crop PDF:

  1. Upload a PDF to Crop PDF.
  2. Enter top, bottom, left, and right margins in points.
  3. Choose whether to apply the margins to all, odd, or even pages.
  4. Select Crop PDF and inspect the resulting page edges.

Step 3 of 7 Target Dropdown (#applyTo)

Choose Target Pages

What to do:

Select whether to crop "All pages", "First page only", or "Current page".

What you will see:

The selector ensures crop margins apply consistently across your target range.

Next:

Select "All pages" for scanned book chapters or uniform documents.

Step 4 of 7 Margin Inputs (#top, #bottom, #left, #right)

Enter Margin Trim Values

What to do:

Enter the points to trim from the Top, Bottom, Left, and Right edges (e.g. 36pt = 0.5 inch).

What you will see:

The input fields register trim depths for each border.

Next:

Verify that trim values do not cut into actual body text.

Tip:

72 points equals 1 inch (25.4mm). A 36pt trim removes exactly half an inch.

Tips & Best Practices

  • Use the preview dimensions shown on the page to estimate points.
  • Start with small margins and verify that headers and footers remain visible.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Entering margins larger than the page dimensions.
  • Confusing cropping with deleting content; the original content may still exist outside the crop box.

Troubleshooting & FAQ

If the output is blank or clipped, retry with smaller margins.

If the file cannot be read, use a valid, unencrypted PDF.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I crop individual pages?

This page applies the setting by page parity, not individually.

Does cropping remove content permanently?

It changes visible bounds rather than redrawing content.
