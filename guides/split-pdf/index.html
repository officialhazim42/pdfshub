Split PDF Guide & Walkthrough

Create separate PDFs from ranges, every page, or groups of N pages.

Tool Overview & Requirements

Split PDF copies selected page groups into separate files. It supports explicit ranges, one file per page, and every-N-pages grouping.

Input One PDF and split settings
Output One or more PDF downloads
Requirements One readable PDF is required. Multiple groups can trigger multiple browser downloads.

Step-by-Step Instructions

Follow these essential instructions to achieve optimal results when using Split PDF:

  1. Upload a PDF and choose a split mode.
  2. Enter ranges or a group size when the chosen mode needs it.
  3. Select Split PDF and wait for each group to be created.
  4. Open the resulting files and verify their names and page ranges.

Step 1 of 7 Upload Drop Zone (#drop)

Open Split PDF

What to do:

Navigate to Split PDF and locate the upload zone.

What you will see:

The upload area accepts any multi-page PDF document.

Next:

Click to select or drag and drop your PDF file.

Tip:

Ensure your document contains at least two pages to enable splitting options.

Step 2 of 7 File Browser (#fin)

Upload Multi-Page PDF

What to do:

Select your target document from your local files.

What you will see:

The file name and total detected page count are displayed above the options panel.

Next:

Proceed to configure how you want the pages divided.

Tip:

The tool reads document page counts instantly without transmitting files over the network.

Step 3 of 7 Mode Selector (#mode)

Choose Split Mode

What to do:

Select your preferred division method from the "Split mode" dropdown.

What you will see:

Options include "Extract page ranges", "Split every page into separate files", or "Split every N pages".

Next:

Choose "Extract page ranges" to specify custom sections.

Tip:

Choosing "Split every page" creates individual single-page documents for every sheet.

Step 4 of 7 Range Input (#ranges)

Enter Target Page Ranges

What to do:

Type the specific page ranges you want to extract into the input field.

What you will see:

Input accepts comma-separated pages and hyphens for ranges (e.g. "1-2, 3").

Next:

Double check that your numbers do not exceed the total page count.

Tip:

You can create multiple separate output files in one go by separating ranges with commas.

Step 5 of 7 Split Action Button (#splitBtn)

Click Split PDF

What to do:

Click the primary "Split PDF" action button.

What you will see:

The status line shows processing activity as pages are parsed into separate documents.

Next:

Wait a moment while the client-side engine slices the document.

Tip:

The original document remains completely untouched on your device.

Step 6 of 7 Generated Preview (#generatedPreview)

Review Split Pages

What to do:

Review the generated output document in the preview frame.

What you will see:

The preview displays the newly isolated page sections for verification.

Next:

Click "Continue to download" once you verify the extracted pages.

Tip:

Ensure that no necessary pages were omitted before saving.

Step 7 of 7 Download Modal (#dlModal .btn-success)

Download Split Files

What to do:

Click the green "Download" button to save your files.

What you will see:

A confirmation dialog appears offering the extracted PDF or a ZIP archive containing all parts.

Next:

Save the file to your computer or cloud drive.

Tip:

When multiple ranges are generated, they are conveniently packaged into a clean ZIP archive.

Tips & Best Practices

  • Use explicit ranges when the output groups need different sizes.
  • Allow multiple downloads in the browser for multi-file exports.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving ranges empty in range mode.
  • Choosing Every page for a very large document without expecting many downloads.

Troubleshooting & FAQ

If no files are created, correct the ranges or N value.

If downloads are blocked, update the browser permission for this site.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I split every page separately?

Yes, choose Every page as separate PDF.

Does the original remain intact?

Yes.
